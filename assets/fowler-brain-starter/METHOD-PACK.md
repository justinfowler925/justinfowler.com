# Sales methods

Package: fowler-brain-sales-starter · Version: 0.1.0 · Pilot candidate

These are proposed working methods. They require company-specific calibration and evaluation.

## Shared evidence convention

Use these labels when they help the reader distinguish authority:

- **Verified fact:** supported by the identified responsible source, with date or retrieval time.
- **Buyer statement:** what a named speaker said, with the meeting or message reference. This verifies the statement was made, not necessarily the underlying claim.
- **Hypothesis:** an interpretation to test; include the evidence and a competing explanation.
- **Unknown:** material information unavailable or unresolved.
- **Proposed action:** our suggestion; never present it as a buyer commitment.

For conflicting facts, identify the two sources and their scope. Prefer the authority responsible for the particular fact, considering its effective date and possible supersession. If that cannot be established, leave the conflict open. A newer document is not automatically authoritative.

## Meeting preparation

Trigger: prepare for an account meeting, executive conversation, or discovery call.

Inputs: account, participants, meeting purpose and date; permitted account sources; approved product references if product claims are needed. Missing product documentation means no affirmative product promise.

Procedure:

1. State the decision this meeting should advance. Use the account packet and latest relevant records. Verify the account and connector identity before interpreting an empty search.
2. Select up to five material facts or buyer statements. Give each a supporting source and date. Distinguish public context from information confirmed by this buyer.
3. Form up to three useful hypotheses. Include one credible alternative explanation and the question that would discriminate between them.
4. Draft five prioritized discovery questions connected to the evidence, not five generic questions detached from the account.
5. Recommend one realistic meeting outcome and next step. Leave owner and date unconfirmed if not agreed.
6. Inspect the finished brief for unsupported capabilities, invented stakeholders, implied budget, and stale commitments.

Output: the meeting brief in TEMPLATES.md; target one readable page unless the user needs more.

Acceptance: every material fact is sourced; hypotheses remain distinct; missing budget/timing stays unknown; no invented product fit; questions are relevant; the requested meeting objective is addressed.

## Discovery synthesis

Trigger: turn notes or a transcript into an account update and recommended next step.

Inputs: identified meeting source and date; speaker names if known; existing account context and approved qualification definitions if available.

Procedure:

1. Extract the buyer's stated problem, impact, decision process, constraints, and desired outcome. Mark absent dimensions unknown.
2. Separate direct statements from our interpretation. Do not manufacture exact quotes from a paraphrase. Attach transcript timestamps or note references where available.
3. Compare with prior account context. Identify what changed, what was contradicted, and what remains untested.
4. List actual commitments by owner and due date. Use “not agreed” where needed.
5. Recommend the next discovery or validation step. If a qualification field is suggested, include supporting evidence and keep it a proposal until the authorized record update occurs.

Output: discovery summary plus a proposed account update. Do not update the CRM unless authorized and available.

Acceptance: speaker attribution is supported; no inferred budget, champion, or procurement date becomes fact; conflicts are visible; commitments match the source; next steps have a clear purpose.

## Follow-up draft

Trigger: draft an email or message after a meeting.

Inputs: reviewed discovery summary; intended recipients; actual commitments; desired next step; tone or length preferences.

Procedure:

1. Briefly reflect the buyer's stated objective in ordinary language.
2. Include only commitments actually made. Put suggestions in conditional language.
3. Request one clear next step. Do not insert a date as agreed if it was not agreed.
4. Check product promises against approved evidence. If a response requires confirmation, say it needs confirmation.
5. Produce the draft, followed by internal review notes listing sources, unresolved claims, and any missing recipient details. Keep internal notes outside the sendable body.

Output: subject and sendable body, plus separate internal review notes. Sending requires actual authorization and an available tool.

Acceptance: buyer intent is represented accurately; no unsupported guarantee, fabricated ROI, or invented commitment; the next step is concrete; confidential internal notes are not part of the customer message.

## Improvement loop

After a correction, propose a lesson: situation, observed error, evidence, affected method, proposed change, counterexample, and validation case. An owner reviews scope and accepts or rejects it. Add accepted changes to the maintained source, release a new version, and re-test affected destinations.

When the user is learning, ask one short question about the evidence or an alternative explanation. Do not delay an urgent deliverable with an unrequested training exercise.
