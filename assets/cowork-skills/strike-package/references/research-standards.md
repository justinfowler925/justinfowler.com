# Research and Evidence Standards

Use these standards whenever researching or refreshing a Strike Package.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. official employer, government, regulator, court, registry, filing, or legislative records;
2. the subject’s own attributable speech, testimony, interview, article, or verified professional profile;
3. reputable reporting with named authors, dates, and direct evidence;
4. established conference, professional association, academic, or publisher biographies; and
5. secondary databases or aggregators only as discovery aids or when better evidence does not exist.

Do not cite search-result snippets, AI summaries, people-search sites, scraped biography farms, unsourced reposts, or anonymous claims as proof. Social posts can prove only what the attributable account posted; they do not independently prove the underlying claim.

Use a primary source plus independent corroboration for identity-critical facts when practical. For contentious, adverse, or reputation-sensitive claims, use multiple high-quality sources and describe allegations, findings, denials, and procedural status precisely.

## Identity resolution

Before collecting biography facts, match at least two stable identifiers such as employer, title, geography, education, career history, photograph on an official page, or an attributable biography. Do not merge records based on name alone. Record unresolved ambiguity in the verification notes.

## Claim-source ledger

Maintain a working ledger with:

- exact claim;
- classification: verified fact, client-provided context, assessment, or hypothesis;
- source title and publisher;
- publication or last-updated date when available;
- direct URL;
- access date;
- supporting passage or location for internal checking; and
- confidence or conflict note.

For LinkedIn and other restricted sources, also record access class (public, account-visible, or user-supplied), acquisition method, original author, content type, and any audience restriction. Keep access class separate from confidence: account visibility does not independently corroborate a claim. Label user-supplied excerpts or exports as client-provided unless independently inspected or corroborated. Follow [linkedin-workflow.md](linkedin-workflow.md).

The supporting passage is research working material; do not reproduce long copyrighted excerpts in the final brief.

## Currency and conflicts

Use an explicit research cutoff date. Current-role claims require a current official source or two recent credible sources. Preserve effective dates for transitions and distinguish announced, nominated, interim, former, and current roles.

When sources conflict, prefer the source with direct authority and the more recent update, but disclose material conflicts. Never silently select the version that makes the meeting strategy more compelling.

## Fact, assessment, and hypothesis

A citation does not turn an inference into a fact. Use labels such as:

- **Verified:** the source directly supports the claim.
- **Assessment:** the cited facts support this interpretation, but the source does not state it.
- **Question to test:** the available evidence makes this worth asking; the answer is unknown.
- **Client-provided:** supplied by the user and not independently verified.

Avoid unsupported psychographic claims such as “will respond to,” “thinks in terms of,” “is a gatekeeper,” or “will offer introductions.” Convert them to evidence-based observations, qualified assessments, or questions.

## Citation format

Place a numbered clickable citation immediately after the supported claim: `[1]`. Reuse a number when the same source supports multiple claims. In tables, put citations in the relevant cell or row.

End with a numbered source register containing:

`[1] Page or document title — Publisher/author, publication date or n.d.; accessed YYYY-MM-DD. Direct URL.`

For PDFs, recordings, long transcripts, or filings, add page, timestamp, section, or paragraph detail when available. Preserve full URLs as hyperlinks in `.docx` output.

For account-visible sources, add “LinkedIn — account-visible; recipient access may be required.” For supplied exports or excerpts without a permalink, cite a descriptive source identifier and item location, with the supplied date. Never invent a URL or call restricted material public. Include only minimal excerpts needed to support the brief.

## Privacy and professional relevance

Use lawfully accessible public information and professional information the user is authorized to access and use, limited to what is relevant to the meeting. Account-visible LinkedIn professional content and connections can be used within that scope; visibility is not permission for unrestricted redistribution. Exclude leaked or unauthorized material, data-broker records, personal contact information, exact location patterns, family details, protected-class information, and unrelated personal history. Availability alone does not make a fact relevant. Do not include private messages by default; use only relevant material explicitly supplied or authorized for this task and preserve applicable audience restrictions.

Do not infer sensitive traits, security clearances, political beliefs, health, criminality, financial distress, or personal vulnerabilities. Do not recommend manipulation, coercion, intimidation, impersonation, or exploiting personal circumstances. Rapport suggestions must be respectful, professional, and based on genuinely shared or public professional interests.
