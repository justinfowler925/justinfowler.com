# Your Best Working Methods Should Travel With Your Team

Builders have learned to give AI a working environment: context, reusable procedures, tools, examples, and checks before delivery.

Salespeople deserve that environment too.

My position is that our best working methods should become maintained assets that people can use inside their own Claude projects and sessions. I call my version Fowler Brain. This is the standard I think we should build toward, including where my own setup needs to improve.

## There is evidence for the opportunity—and for testing it

A published study of 5,172 customer-support agents found that AI assistance increased issues resolved per hour by about 15% on average, with greater benefits for less experienced workers. That supports the possibility of distributing useful expertise. It does not establish a sales productivity forecast. [The study](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658).

The BCG consultant experiment supplies the other half of the argument: AI improved performance on some tasks, but users were 19 percentage points less likely to solve one business problem outside its capabilities correctly. [The study](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf).

We need a system that makes good methods easier to apply and poor answers easier to catch. Copying a builder's favorite configuration is only a starting hypothesis.

## Separate the method, the account, and the live facts

I would maintain one shared library of approved methods, templates, examples, and reviewed lessons. Every asset gets an owner, version, source, and review date.

Each account workspace adds the context its users are authorized to see. Current opportunity state and commercial commitments remain in their responsible systems. A remembered value has a date; it does not automatically become today's truth.

The reusable methods should travel widely. Account information should follow its permission boundaries.

## Package decisions people can use

Start with three jobs: meeting preparation, discovery synthesis, and the follow-up.

For each, capture the outcome, required inputs, steps, deliverable, and acceptance checks. Include an annotated good example and a plausible bad one. Explain the distinction.

A meeting brief should connect verified evidence to useful questions. A follow-up should distinguish a buyer commitment from our proposed next step. A confident account hypothesis should remain a hypothesis until the buyer supplies evidence.

This translates builder discipline into sales work: clarify the outcome, inspect the source, use a repeatable method, create an artifact, verify it, and capture a correction.

Skills are one useful packaging mechanism. Anthropic describes them as instructions and supporting resources that compatible agents can load for relevant tasks. A skill supplies procedure; connectors supply access; templates specify outputs. Teams need to install and test the complete combination. [Agent Skills](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills).

## Make portability observable

Put a short working agreement in Claude Project instructions, with approved methods in Project knowledge. Install relevant skills where the workspace supports them. Keep the detailed library available on demand.

Then test a fresh session. Can it use a distinctive rule from the current package? Can another project use the same method? Can the next session resume from a saved handoff? Does an update change behavior? Does a restricted account stay restricted?

Claude documents separate project memory spaces. Cowork and Claude Code also have their own setup mechanisms. We should verify each destination instead of assuming a universal memory layer. [Claude memory](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273-use-claude-s-chat-search-and-memory-to-build-on-previous-context); [Cowork projects](https://claude.com/docs/cowork/guide/projects); [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory).

## Give the rep a way to learn

Producing a better document and building better judgment are different outcomes.

A small coding-learning experiment found lower subsequent quiz scores among AI-assisted participants. It cannot tell us the effect on sales learning. It does justify checking comprehension separately from output quality. [The research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills).

Ask the rep to explain the strongest source, the weakest assumption, and the question that could change the recommendation. After the meeting, compare the hypothesis with the buyer's actual words. Capture what should change in the method.

## Earn the rollout

Pilot the package against both the current workflow and a strong basic Claude setup. Measure total time through review, factual errors, unsupported claims, correction effort, and usefulness of the next step. Keep model and source conditions comparable. Save failed examples as regression tests.

Keep the parts that improve the work. Revise or remove the parts that add ceremony.

I want our salespeople to inherit the practical advantages of a well-built AI working environment: trusted context, usable methods, relevant tools, continuity, and a clear standard of evidence.

Fowler Brain should make that possible across the surfaces people use. Its success should be visible in their work.
